# The Secret Life of a Banana

## A Journey Through Farms, Oceans, Markets, and the Hidden Systems Behind Everyday Food

A banana on a kitchen counter looks simple. Its journey is anything but.

This illustrated book follows one familiar fruit backward and forward through the
world that makes it possible: from a tropical plant, through a farm and packing
house, into refrigerated containers and across an ocean, then through ports,
ripening rooms, highways, grocery stores, kitchens, and—finally—soil or landfill.

At the center of the book is one question:

> **How does a banana travel from a tropical plant to a kitchen thousands of
> miles away, and what hidden systems make that journey possible?**

## What this book explores

The banana is our guide to a connected story of:

- plant biology, tropical weather, soil, and agricultural practice;
- the skill and labor behind growing, cutting, washing, sorting, and packing;
- refrigeration, containers, ships, ports, warehouses, trucks, and timing;
- prices, trade, grocery displays, household choices, and food culture;
- peels, waste, compost, microorganisms, and renewed growth; and
- systems thinking: how distant people, places, materials, and decisions meet in
  one everyday object.

The story will be accurate without becoming an encyclopedia. Each chapter will
focus on a small number of memorable ideas, use plain language, and make room for
questions, wonder, and the people whose work is often hidden from view.

## Who it is for

The book is for curious general readers, families, classrooms, and anyone who has
ever picked up a banana without imagining the thousands of hands, miles, and
carefully controlled conditions behind it. No specialist background is assumed.

## An illustrated documentary

This is a visual story first: an illustrated documentary that happens to be a
book. Cinematic scenes will move between radically different scales—a bead of
rain on a leaf, a worker guiding a heavy stalk, a refrigerated container in a
vast ship, a bright grocery aisle, and microscopic life transforming a peel.

Illustrations should remain grounded in real people, environments, tools,
machines, food, and movement. Diagrams may clarify a physical process when
useful, but computer screens, dashboards, source code, generic offices, and
abstract technology are not the visual center of this world. See the
[visual style guide](docs/VISUAL_STYLE.md) for the shared art direction.

## Read the journey

The book is planned as 13 intentionally concise chapters in five parts:

1. **Born in the Tropics** — encounter the fruit, its climate, and its growth.
2. **The Journey Begins** — follow harvest, cooling, packing, and ocean travel.
3. **Arriving in America** — enter the port, ripening room, and regional network.
4. **The Banana We Know** — consider price, retail, and the moment of eating.
5. **After the Banana** — follow the peel and see the whole system anew.
