# Visual Style

## Guiding idea

Treat every chapter as a short cinematic documentary. The images should make an
ordinary banana feel immediate, physical, and astonishing while remaining
faithful to real places, work, biology, and infrastructure.

## The visual world

- **Nature has texture.** Show humid air, heavy rain, luminous leaves, bruised
  fruit, rich soil, hard sunlight, water droplets, roots, insects, and decay.
- **People are participants, not scenery.** Portray agricultural, packing-house,
  port, warehouse, transport, retail, and kitchen work with specificity and
  dignity. Favor observed action over anonymous poses.
- **Machines have weight.** Convey cables, blades, wash tanks, conveyors,
  refrigeration equipment, containers, cranes, ships, pallets, and trucks as
  physical things with scale, wear, sound, and purpose.
- **Places feel distinct.** Move from lush farms to cool warehouses, immense
  ports, open ocean, busy highways, colorful produce aisles, intimate kitchens,
  and living compost.
- **Scale tells the story.** Alternate extreme close-ups—flowers, pores, fibers,
  condensation, microorganisms—with wide landscapes and global transport
  systems. Let a single fruit remain the thread between them.

## Image approach

Each chapter should eventually open with one strong **hero illustration** in
`images/chapters/`. Favor documentary realism with painterly warmth, cinematic
composition, natural gestures, atmospheric depth, and a clear focal point.
Supporting images may use close observation, cutaways, sequences, or simple
maps when they illuminate a physical journey.

Use rich tropical greens, rain-dark earth, ocean blues, industrial steel and
sodium light, refrigerated whites, road asphalt, grocery color, kitchen warmth,
and the changing green-to-yellow-to-brown palette of the banana itself. Lighting
should help carry the journey: humid dawn, hard harvest daylight, cold storage,
port night, supermarket brightness, and quiet morning sun.

## Accuracy and care

Research the location, season, cultivar, clothing, tools, safety equipment,
handling method, vehicle, and machinery before illustrating them. Avoid blending
unrelated regions into a generic “tropics.” Show labor without romanticizing
hardship or reducing workers to symbols. Images should clarify interdependence,
not imply that a complex supply chain is effortless or invisible by magic.

## What not to center

Do not make computer screens, dashboards, charts, source code, control rooms, or
generic office scenes the dominant imagery. Technology belongs when it is a real
part of the physical system—such as a refrigeration sensor or crane control—but
the visual identity stays rooted in **people, places, nature, machinery,
transportation, food, and landscapes**.

## Rhythm

Keep chapters visually generous and intentionally concise. Pair one memorable
scene with a few carefully chosen details rather than crowding every page. Across
the book, repeat visual motifs—the blue sticker, the curve of the fruit, hands,
water, boxes, and circles of growth and decay—to connect distant stages of the
same journey.
